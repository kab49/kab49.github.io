Bromus erectus

Upright Brome

Bromus erectus, commonly known as upright brome or meadow brome, is an exceptionally resilient, densely tufted perennial grass that plays a key role in stabilizing open soils and establishing vibrant wildflower meadows. Renowned for its tall, erect carriage and striking, compact clusters of slender spikelets, this versatile grass stands as a robust symbol of endurance across diverse temperate terrains.

Historically originating in Eurasia and North Africa, species within the Bromus genus share deep structural and historical lineages with ancient cereal crops like wheat and barley. While primarily utilized globally as a reliable forage grass and structural companion in pasture landscapes, traditional land-management practices have long valued its robust, deep fibrous root systems for binding fragile soils and mitigating erosion across human-disturbed environments.

Plant Data

Names and Taxonomy

  • Common Names: “Upright Brome, Meadow Brome, Erect Brome”
  • Synonyms: “Bromopsis erecta (Huds.) Fourr.”
  • Family: Poaceae
  • Genus Information: CA: 31, NV: 22, AZ: 18, UT: 25, NM: 20, CO: 26

Habit and Size

  • Duration: Perennial
  • Type: Grass
  • Habit: Erect
  • Shape: Irregular
  • Size: 24-47 in x 4-20 in (60-120 cm x 10-50 cm)
  • Stem: Erect, slender, unbranched culms; green, often slightly hairy or puberulent near the nodes

Leaves

  • Color: Bright green to deep green
  • Shape: Linear, flat, or slightly folded
  • Edge: Entire, finely pubescent along the margins
  • Ligule-Petiole:
  • Arrangement: Alternate
  • Leaf Retention: Deciduous

Flowers and Fruit

  • Sex: Hermaphroditic
  • Flower Color: Green, sometimes tinged with purple or bronze
  • Flower Size: Spikelets 1.5–3 cm long
  • Number of Petals: Absent (reduced to palea and lemma structures)
  • Bloom: June–July
  • Fruit: Caryopsis (dry, one-seeded grain)

Habitat and Growing Conditions

  • Water Use: Moderate
  • Light: Full Sun
  • Soil: Calcareous, rocky, well-draining, or humus-rich soils
  • Habitat: Fields, meadows, roadsides, and human-disturbed anthropogenic habitats

Distribution

  • Distribution: Native to Europe, western Asia, and northern Africa; naturalized across portions of North America
  • Seen: Location or date of observation
  • Elevation: 4,000 ft (1,219 m) or above
