Clustered Field Sedge

Clustered Field Sedge

An incredibly resilient and adaptable graminoid, Carex praegracilis is celebrated for its ability to thrive across challenging landscapes, from alkaline salt meadows to disturbed rangelands. Known for its dense, creeping dark rhizomes, this tough sedge naturally weaves a sturdy underground net that forms protective clonal turfs. Its exceptional tolerance to foot traffic, brackish conditions, and drought makes it an ecologically invaluable turf-alternative and a primary guardian against erosion in native wetlands.

Indigenous groups across its range relied on the fibrous qualities of sedges for practical utility. Though Carex barbarae is the most famously documented 'white root' basketry material among California tribes, Carex praegracilis was similarly integrated into the construction of coiled or woven baskets, mats, and cordage. Its tough, extensive rhizomes and dense leaves provided a dependable material base for daily domestic containers and functional structures.

Plant Data

Names and Taxonomy

  • Common Names: “Clustered Field Sedge, Slim Sedge, Field Sedge, Tollgate Sedge”
  • Synonyms: “Carex camporum, Carex marcida, Carex usta, Carex latebrosa”
  • Family: Cyperaceae
  • Genus Information: CA: ~145 species, AZ: ~35 species, NV: ~45 species, UT: ~90 species, NM: ~50 species

Habit and Size

  • Duration: Perennial
  • Type: Grass
  • Habit: Erect
  • Shape: Irregular
  • Size: 10 to 40 in (25 to 100 cm) x 1 to 2 ft (30 to 60 cm)
  • Stem: Culms are sharply triangular, slender, smooth or slightly roughened below the inflorescence, growing from tough, creeping, blackish-brown rhizomes

Leaves

  • Color: gray-green to dark green
  • Shape: Linear
  • Edge: Entire
  • Ligule-Petiole:
  • Arrangement: Alternate
  • Leaf Retention: Semi-evergreen to Evergreen

Flowers and Fruit

  • Sex: Dioecious / Monoecious
  • Flower Color: Greenish-brown to dark brown
  • Flower Size: Inflorescence 0.4 to 2 in (1 to 5 cm) long
  • Number of Petals: Absent (flowers are reduced and subtended by brown scales)
  • Bloom: May–August
  • Fruit: Perigynia enclosing a lenticular, brown achene

Habitat and Growing Conditions

  • Water Use: Moderate
  • Light: Full Sun / Part Shade
  • Soil: Sandy / Clay / Alkaline / Saline / Moist
  • Habitat: Wet meadows, alkaline flats, riparian areas, seeps, and seasonal marshes

Distribution

  • Distribution: Widespread throughout North America, including the Southwest United States, extending from lowlands to montane life zones
  • Seen: Observed in wet meadow habitats and alkaline flats within the Mojave region
  • Elevation: 0 ft (0 m) to 11,000 ft (3,353 m)
