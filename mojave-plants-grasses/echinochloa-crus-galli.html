Barnyard Grass

An incredibly resilient and adaptable species, Echinochloa crus-galli—commonly known as barnyard grass—stands out as one of the world's most successful wild cereal grasses. Renowned for its rapid growth cycle and high seed yield, this robust plant mimics agricultural crops so effectively that it thrives as both a cosmopolitan weed in rice paddies and an occasional nutritional safeguard during historical crop failures.

Historically, tribal communities and rural healers turned to barnyard grass during periods of severe drought and famine, utilizing its nutrient-dense grains to make survival porridges. Additionally, traditional Ayurvedic and Unani practitioners valued the grain endosperm for its soothing, low-glycemic, and mild diuretic properties, often incorporating it into localized folk medicine to treat digestive ailments and manage blood sugar levels.

Plant Data

Names and Taxonomy

  • Common Names: “Barnyardgrass, Barnyard Millet, Watergrass, Cockspur Grass, Japanese Millet”
  • Synonyms: “Panicum crus-galli, Echinochloa crusgalli”
  • Family: Poaceae
  • Genus Information: CA: 5, NV: 2, AZ: 4, UT: 3, NM: 4

Habit and Size

  • Duration: Annual
  • Type: “Grass, Weed”
  • Habit: Erect / Decumbent
  • Shape: Open / Spreading
  • Size: 1–5 ft x 1–2 ft
  • Stem: Stout, flattened at the base, often branching; bases are frequently reddish to dark purple

Leaves

  • Color: Bright green to purplish-striped
  • Shape: Linear, flat, elongated
  • Edge: Entire, slightly rough
  • Ligule-Petiole:
  • Arrangement: Alternate
  • Leaf Retention: Deciduous

Flowers and Fruit

  • Sex: Hermaphroditic
  • Flower Color: Green to reddish-purple
  • Flower Size: 4–10 in long panicles
  • Number of Petals: Lacks true petals; spikelets are crowded with stiff hairs and variable awns
  • Bloom: July–November
  • Fruit: Caryopsis (dry grain) tightly bound by chaff

Habitat and Growing Conditions

  • Water Use: Moderate / High
  • Light: Full Sun
  • Soil: Alluvial, moist, sandy, or heavy clay soils; highly tolerant of flooding
  • Habitat: Ditches, fields, roadsides, disturbed wet waste areas, and lake shores

Distribution

  • Distribution: Widespread throughout temperate and tropical regions globally; naturalized throughout the continental United States
  • Seen: Moist lowlands and cultivated margins
  • Elevation: 0 ft (0 m) to 8,200 ft (2,500 m)
