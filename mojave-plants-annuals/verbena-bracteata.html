Verbena bracteata

Bigbract Vervain

Verbena bracteata, commonly known as bigbract vervain or prostrate vervain, is a rugged, low-growing native wildflower characterized by its mat-forming growth habit and prominent, leaf-like floral bracts that extend beyond its tiny lavender to purple flowers. Extremely adaptable and drought-hardy, it thrives in dry, disturbed soils and open, sun-baked desert environments where few other plants can establish.

Historically, various Native American tribes utilized Verbena bracteata for a range of medicinal applications. Infusions and decoctions made from the leaves and roots were used by the Navajo and Lakota to treat stomach ailments, fevers, and headaches, while crushed leafy pastes were applied topically as poultices to relieve skin irritations and minor wounds.

Plant Data

Names and Taxonomy

  • Common Names: Bigbract Vervain, Prostrate Vervain, Carpet Vervain, Largebract Vervain
  • Synonyms: Verbena bracteosa, Verbena confinis
  • Family: Verbenaceae
  • Genus Information: Number of Species: AZ: 17, CA: 15, NV: 5, UT: 5, NM: 13, TX: 21

Habit and Size

  • Duration: Annual, Perennial
  • Type: Weed
  • Habit: Sprawling / Prostrate
  • Shape: Prostrate
  • Size: 2–8 inch x 1–3 ft
  • Stem: Hairy, square in cross-section, highly branched, spreading flat along the ground

Leaves

  • Color: gray-green
  • Shape: Lobed, Pinnatifid, Ovate
  • Edge: Serrated, Deeply Lobed
  • Ligule-Petiole:
  • Arrangement: Opposite
  • Leaf Retention: Deciduous

Flowers and Fruit

  • Sex: Hermaphroditic
  • Flower Color: Lavender to Blue-Violet
  • Flower Size: 1/8 to 1/4 inch diameter
  • Number of Petals: 5-lobed, salverform corolla
  • Bloom: May–October
  • Fruit: 4 nutlets enclosed within persistent calyx

Habitat and Growing Conditions

  • Water Use: Low
  • Light: Full Sun
  • Soil: Sandy / Rocky / Disturbed / Well-draining
  • Habitat: Roadsides, desert washes, disturbed flats, waste places

Distribution

  • Distribution: Widespread across North America, including the southwestern United States and Mojave Desert
  • Seen: Mount Charleston, NV
  • Elevation: 500 to 7,500 ft (152 to 2,286 m)
