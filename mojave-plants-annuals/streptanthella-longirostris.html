Streptanthella longirostris

Longbeak Streptanthella

Streptanthella longirostris, commonly known as the longbeak streptanthella or longbeaked twistflower, stands out as a fascinating winter-spring ephemeral of North American deserts. As a monotypic genus, it represents a solitary evolutionary lineage uniquely adapted to thriving on sandy desert washes, flats, and open shrublands where its distinctive reflexed fruits and prolonged beaks help secure its next generation before the onset of extreme summer heat.

Indigenous groups in the Southwest historically utilized the plant for medicinal purposes. Specifically, a decoction of the plant was taken traditionally to alleviate kidney and bladder troubles, and the crushed fresh plants were rubbed on chickenpox sores to soothe itching and promote healing.

Plant Data

Names and Taxonomy

  • Common Names: “Longbeak Streptanthella, Longbeaked Twistflower, Beaked Sandcress, Fiddle Mustard”
  • Synonyms: “Streptanthus longirostris, Streptanthella longirostris var. derelicta”
  • Family: Brassicaceae
  • Genus Information: CA: 1, NV: 1, AZ: 1, UT: 1

Habit and Size

  • Duration: Annual
  • Type: Herb
  • Habit: Erect
  • Shape: Irregular
  • Size: 4 in to 20 in x 3 in to 8 in (10–50 cm tall)
  • Stem: Glabrous, slender, often branched from the base or middle, pale green to glaucous

Leaves

  • Color: glaucous, gray-green
  • Shape: Linear, Lanceolate, to Oblong
  • Edge: Entire to dentate
  • Ligule-Petiole:
  • Arrangement: Alternate
  • Leaf Retention: Deciduous

Flowers and Fruit

  • Sex: Hermaphroditic
  • Flower Color: White, cream, to pale yellow or purple-tinged
  • Flower Size: 1/8 in to 1/4 in long
  • Number of Petals: 4 linear to oblong petals
  • Bloom: March–May
  • Fruit: Reflexed silique (pod) with a conspicuous long beak at the tip

Habitat and Growing Conditions

  • Water Use: Low
  • Light: Full Sun
  • Soil: Sandy / Rocky / Well-draining
  • Habitat: Wash / Slope / Creosote bush scrub / Desert dunes

Distribution

  • Distribution: Western United States including California, Nevada, Arizona, Utah, Oregon, Washington, Wyoming, Colorado, and New Mexico, extending into Baja California, Mexico
  • Seen: Mojave Desert, sandy washes and flats
  • Elevation: 150 ft (46 m) to 7,500 ft (2,286 m)
