Chaenactis stevioides

Desert Pincushion, Esteve's Pincushion

Among the most abundant and cheerful spring wildflowers of the Mojave Desert and Great Basin, Chaenactis stevioides produces delicate, cushion-like white flower heads that carpet arid washes, gravelly flats, and sandy slopes following winter rains.

Indigenous peoples such as the Navajo (Kayenta) historically utilized the sticky juice of the plant as an adhesive to mend broken ceremonial items.

Plant Data

Names and Taxonomy

  • Common Names: Desert Pincushion, Esteve's Pincushion, Steve's Dustymaiden
  • Synonyms: Chaenactis gillespiei, Chaenactis latifolia, Chaenactis mexicana
  • Family: Asteraceae
  • Genus Information: Number of Species: AZ 6, CA 13, NV 8, NM 3, TX 0, UT 5

Habit and Size

  • Duration: Annual
  • Type: Weed
  • Habit: Erect
  • Shape: Irregular
  • Size: 18 inches x 12 inches
  • Stem: Hairy with cobwebby fibers that thin with age, green to reddish brown

Leaves

  • Color: gray-green
  • Shape: Pinnately lobed, divided into several linear segments
  • Edge: Lobed
  • Ligule-Petiole:
  • Arrangement: Alternate
  • Leaf Retention: Deciduous

Flowers and Fruit

  • Sex: Hermaphroditic
  • Flower Color: White, pale cream, outer florets enlarged and ray-like
  • Flower Size: 0.75 inches
  • Number of Petals: 5 lobes per tubular disc floret
  • Bloom: March to June
  • Fruit: Achene with a scaly pappus

Habitat and Growing Conditions

  • Water Use: Low
  • Light: Full Sun
  • Soil: Sandy, Rocky, Well-draining
  • Habitat: Wash, Slope, Creosote bush scrub, Open desert flats

Distribution

  • Distribution: Western United States, AZ, CA, CO, ID, NM, NV, OR, UT, WY, and northwestern Mexico
  • Seen: Lake Mead, NV
  • Elevation: 100 ft (30 m) to 6,500 ft (1,981 m)
