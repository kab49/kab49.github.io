Oenothera podocarpa

Harlequin Bush

Oenothera podocarpa, commonly known as the Harlequin Bush, is a captivating and resilient desert wildflower that graces the arid landscapes of the American Southwest. Formally recognized under its updated classification from the Gaura genus, this plant features uniquely delicate asymmetrical flowers that unfurl near sunset. The evening blooms act as a crucial ecological beacon, transforming into pale pink hues by morning to attract crepuscular pollinators while showcasing nature's adaptive artistry under harsh desert conditions.

Historically, various indigenous groups across the Southwest and Northern Mexico utilized members of the evening primrose and Gaura lineages for both functional and medicinal applications. Infusions made from the roots and leaves were traditionally prepared as poultices to treat inflammation, accelerate the healing of minor wounds, or soothe respiratory ailments. Additionally, the plants held cultural and symbolic value, with their distinctive evening-blooming cycles often woven into indigenous stories and rituals connected with night-pollinating insects.

Plant Data

Names and Taxonomy

  • Common Names: “Harlequin Bush, Harlequin-bush, Long-stipe Bee-blossom”
  • Synonyms: “Gaura hexandra subsp. gracilis, Gaura gracilis, Gaura podocarpa”
  • Family: Onagraceae
  • Genus Information: AZ: 35, NM: 32, UT: 25, NV: 20, CA: 22

Habit and Size

  • Duration: Annual
  • Type: Herb
  • Habit: Erect / Sprawling
  • Shape: Irregular
  • Size: 6 to 26 in x 12 in (15–65 cm x 30 cm)
  • Stem: Branched proximally, slender, short-hirtellous or villous, green to reddish-brown

Leaves

  • Color: bright green, sometimes tinged with red
  • Shape: Lancelolate to Narrowly Ovate
  • Edge: Entire to weakly sinuate-dentate
  • Ligule-Petiole:
  • Arrangement: Alternate
  • Leaf Retention: Deciduous

Flowers and Fruit

  • Sex: Hermaphroditic
  • Flower Color: White, turning pink to reddish as they mature
  • Flower Size: 0.25 to 0.5 inches in diameter
  • Number of Petals: 4 petals, asymmetrical, upward-pointing
  • Bloom: May–September
  • Fruit: Ellipsoid or ovoid capsule, angled, sometimes short-stipitate

Habitat and Growing Conditions

  • Water Use: Low
  • Light: Full Sun / Part Shade
  • Soil: Sandy / Rocky / Well-draining
  • Habitat: Canyons, ravines, rocky slopes, and moist lakeside margins

Distribution

  • Distribution: Arizona, New Mexico, and southward into Mexico
  • Seen: Gila National Forest, New Mexico
  • Elevation: 4,500 ft (1,371 m) to 7,600 ft (2,316 m)
