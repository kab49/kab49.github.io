Mentzelia albicaulis

Whitestem Blazingstar

Mentzelia albicaulis, also known as the whitestem blazingstar, is a fascinating annual desert wildflower known for its distinctive white to ghostly pale stems and brilliant, glistening yellow petals. Beyond its subtle beauty, this resilient survivor thrives in the arid sands and mountain slopes of western North America, using uniquely textured, minutely barbed hairs to grip passing surfaces—a characteristic that makes it as memorable to touch as it is to see.

A highly valued staple among numerous indigenous peoples, the seeds of Mentzelia albicaulis were extensively harvested in early summer. Tribes such as the Cahuilla, Hopi, Paiute, and Kawaiisu parched and ground the tiny, nutrient-dense seeds into a sweet meal or fine flour used to make nourishing porridges and mushes, or kneaded them into a rich seed butter resembling peanut butter. Beyond its nutritional use, the Gosiute applied a salve of pounded seeds as a dressing for severe burns, while the Ramah Navajo utilized a poultice of crushed, soaked seeds to alleviate severe toothaches.

Plant Data

Names and Taxonomy

  • Common Names: “Whitestem Blazingstar, White-stemmed Stickleaf, Small Flowered Blazing Star”
  • Synonyms: “Acrolasia albicaulis, Bartonia albicaulis, Mentzelia albicaulis var. genuina”
  • Family: Loasaceae
  • Genus Information: Number of species in Mentzelia: AZ 28, CA 29, NV 26, NM 20, TX 17, UT 21

Habit and Size

  • Duration: Annual
  • Type: Herb
  • Habit: Erect / Sprawling
  • Shape: Irregular
  • Size: up to 16.5 in (42 cm) tall
  • Stem: Distinctly white or pale, shiny, slender, covered in fine barbed hairs

Leaves

  • Color: green, bright green
  • Shape: Linear to Lanceolate
  • Edge: Lobed (deeply divided into comblike lobes)
  • Ligule-Petiole:
  • Arrangement: Alternate
  • Leaf Retention: Deciduous

Flowers and Fruit

  • Sex: Hermaphroditic
  • Flower Color: Shiny yellow
  • Flower Size: 2 to 7 mm long petals
  • Number of Petals: 5 shiny yellow petals
  • Bloom: March–June
  • Fruit: Narrow, straight or curving utricle/capsule 1 to 3 cm long containing angular bumpy seeds

Habitat and Growing Conditions

  • Water Use: Low
  • Light: Full Sun
  • Soil: Sandy / Rocky / Well-draining
  • Habitat: Wash / Slope / Mountain / Desert and plateau habitats

Distribution

  • Distribution: Western North America
  • Seen: Baker, CA and Gold Butte, NV
  • Elevation: 1,000 ft (305 m) to 4,500 ft (1,372 m)
