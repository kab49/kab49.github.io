Eriogonum hoffmannii

Hoffmann's Buckwheat

Hoffmann's buckwheat is a rare and striking desert annual endemic to the rugged mountain ranges and gravelly slopes surrounding Death Valley. Characterized by its delicate, intricately branched stems and small clusters of pale flowers, this specialized Mojave native thrives in harsh, nutrient-poor desert soils where few other plants can survive.

While specific indigenous uses for Hoffmann's buckwheat are sparsely documented due to its localized range, species of the genus Eriogonum were widely utilized by Southwestern tribes for medicinal teas to treat stomach ailments and colds, as well as ground seeds for nourishment.

Plant Data

Names and Taxonomy

  • Common Names: Hoffmann's Buckwheat, Hoffmann's Wild Buckwheat
  • Synonyms: Eriogonum hoffmannii var. hoffmannii, Eriogonum hoffmannii var. robustius
  • Family: Polygonaceae
  • Genus Information: Number of Species: CA: 115, NV: 62, AZ: 45, UT: 60, NM: 35

Habit and Size

  • Duration: Annual
  • Type: Annual
  • Habit: Erect
  • Shape: Irregular
  • Size: 1' x 1.5' (up to 3 ft tall)
  • Stem: Slender, smooth to slightly glaucous, greenish to reddish-brown, freely branching

Leaves

  • Color: Gray-green to light green
  • Shape: Basal, orbicular to ovate
  • Edge: Entire, wavy or crinkled margins
  • Ligule-Petiole:
  • Arrangement: Basal rosette
  • Leaf Retention: Deciduous

Flowers and Fruit

  • Sex: Hermaphroditic
  • Flower Color: Yellowish to white, often tinged with pink or reddish-brown
  • Flower Size: 1/8 in (2–3 mm)
  • Number of Petals: 6 tepals in two whorls, glabrous
  • Bloom: April–June
  • Fruit: Small 3-angled achene, light brown

Habitat and Growing Conditions

  • Water Use: Low
  • Light: Full Sun
  • Soil: Gravelly, rocky, carbonate, or volcanic soils
  • Habitat: Desert canyons, wash slopes, and rocky hillsides in creosote bush scrub and pinyon-juniper woodland

Distribution

  • Distribution: Endemic to eastern California (Inyo County, Death Valley region) and adjacent Nevada
  • Seen: Panamint Mountains, Death Valley National Park, Inyo County, CA
  • Elevation: 1,000 ft (305 m) to 6,000 ft (1,828 m)
