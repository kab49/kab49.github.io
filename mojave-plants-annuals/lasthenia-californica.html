Lasthenia californica

California Goldfields

California Goldfields is a vibrant, low-growing annual wildflower celebrated for turning expansive hillsides, open fields, and desert margins into sweeping, brilliant carpets of gold each spring. Following adequate winter rainfall, its mass blooms create one of the West's most spectacular floral displays while providing an invaluable early-season nectar source for native pollinators.

Indigenous peoples of California and the Southwest historically harvested the abundant tiny seeds of Lasthenia species. The seeds were parched, ground into a nutritious meal, and blended into pinole or staple seed pastes.

Plant Data

Names and Taxonomy

  • Common Names: California Goldfields, Goldfields, Common Goldfields
  • Synonyms: Baeria californica, Baeria chrysostoma
  • Family: Asteraceae
  • Genus Information: Number of Species: AZ: 1, CA: 15, NM: 1, NV: 0, UT: 0

Habit and Size

  • Duration: Annual
  • Type: Weed
  • Habit: Erect / Sprawling
  • Shape: Rounded / Irregular
  • Size: 2" - 16" x 4" - 6"
  • Stem: Slender, hairy, simple or branching, greenish to reddish

Leaves

  • Color: green, gray-green
  • Shape: Linear
  • Edge: Entire, sometimes slightly lobed
  • Ligule-Petiole:
  • Arrangement: Opposite
  • Leaf Retention: Deciduous

Flowers and Fruit

  • Sex: Hermaphroditic
  • Flower Color: Bright yellow to gold
  • Flower Size: 0.5 - 1 inch diameter
  • Number of Petals: 8 to 13 ray florets around central disc florets
  • Bloom: February–June
  • Fruit: Achene (cypsela)

Habitat and Growing Conditions

  • Water Use: Low
  • Light: Full Sun
  • Soil: Sandy / Clay / Rocky / Well-draining
  • Habitat: Open slopes, grasslands, vernal pool margins, desert scrub

Distribution

  • Distribution: California, Oregon, Arizona, New Mexico, Baja California
  • Seen: Antelope Valley, CA
  • Elevation: 0 - 4,920 ft (0 - 1,500 m)
