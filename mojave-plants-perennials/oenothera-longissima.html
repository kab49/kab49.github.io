Oenothera longissima

Longstem Evening Primrose

Oenothera longissima, commonly known as the longstem or long-tubed evening primrose, is a striking perennial herb native to the southwestern United States. Renowned for its exceptionally long hypanthium tube and large, luminous yellow flowers that open at dusk to attract hawkmoths, it stands as a dramatic sentinel along moist canyon bottoms, seeps, and riparian corridors in arid landscapes.

Indigenous peoples of the Southwest, including Navajo and Hopi tribes, historically utilized various Oenothera species for medicinal applications, preparing poultices from crushed roots and leaves to treat skin swellings, wounds, and sore muscles, while also incorporating the edible seeds and young foliage into seasonal diets.

Plant Data

Names and Taxonomy

  • Common Names: Longstem Evening Primrose, Long-tubed Evening Primrose
  • Synonyms: Oenothera clutei, Oenothera longissima ssp. clutei
  • Family: Onagraceae
  • Genus Information: Number of Species: CA 38, NV 24, AZ 35, UT 32, NM 36, CO 28

Habit and Size

  • Duration: Biennial, Perennial
  • Type: Herb, Weed
  • Habit: Erect
  • Shape: Columnar, Irregular
  • Size: 3 to 8 ft height, 1 to 3 ft width
  • Stem: Green to reddish, glandular-pubescent, erect, stout

Leaves

  • Color: bright green, gray-green
  • Shape: Lanceolate, Linear
  • Edge: Entire, Denticulate
  • Ligule-Petiole:
  • Arrangement: Alternate
  • Leaf Retention: Deciduous

Flowers and Fruit

  • Sex: Hermaphroditic
  • Flower Color: Yellow, fading to orange or reddish pink
  • Flower Size: 2 to 3 inches wide
  • Number of Petals: 4 broad obovate petals
  • Bloom: June–October
  • Fruit: Elongated, erect cylindrical capsule

Habitat and Growing Conditions

  • Water Use: Moderate, High
  • Light: Full Sun, Part Shade
  • Soil: Sandy, Gravelly, Moist well-draining
  • Habitat: Canyon streamsides, Springs, Seeps, Moist washes

Distribution

  • Distribution: Southwestern United States, including California, Nevada, Utah, Arizona, New Mexico, Colorado
  • Seen: Calico Basin, NV
  • Elevation: 3,000 to 8,500 ft (914 to 2,591 m)
