Medicago sativa

Alfalfa

Alfalfa (Medicago sativa) is renowned as the 'Queen of Forages' due to its exceptionally deep taproot system, which can penetrate up to 20 feet into the earth, granting it extraordinary drought resilience. Historically treasured for animal nutrition and human wellness, its vibrant purple-to-yellow flower clusters and coiled pods underscore its status as a cornerstone species of agricultural ingenuity and botanical adaptability.

Originating in ancient Persia, alfalfa was traditionally fed to elite purebred horses and human athletes to build physical strength and reduce systemic acidity. Traditional Chinese, Native American, and Ayurvedic medicine practitioners have historically used it to improve memory, alleviate arthritis, stimulate appetite, and treat kidney, bowel, and fluid retention disorders.

Plant Data

Names and Taxonomy

  • Common Names: “Alfalfa, Lucerne”
  • Synonyms: “Medicago tunetana, Medicago sativa ssp. sativa”
  • Family: Fabaceae
  • Genus Information: AZ: 4, CA: 7, NV: 3, NM: 3, UT: 3, TX: 6

Habit and Size

  • Duration: Perennial
  • Type: “Herb”
  • Habit: Erect / Decumbent
  • Shape: Rounded / Irregular
  • Size: 2' to 3' x Variable
  • Stem: Multiple erect or decumbent stems growing from a thick, woody crown; mostly hairless or with small, soft hairs

Leaves

  • Color: Bright green
  • Shape: Ovate, trifoliate (pinnately compound with the central leaflet on a longer stalk)
  • Edge: Serrated at the tip
  • Ligule-Petiole:
  • Arrangement: Alternate
  • Leaf Retention: Deciduous

Flowers and Fruit

  • Sex: Hermaphroditic
  • Flower Color: Purple, blue, greenish-yellow, or multicolored
  • Flower Size: 13 mm (approx. 0.5 inches) long
  • Number of Petals: 5 petals arranged into a standard pea-like banner, wings, and keel structure
  • Bloom: April–October
  • Fruit: Spirally-coiled or sickle-type pods enclosing several small, kidney-shaped seeds

Habitat and Growing Conditions

  • Water Use: Low (Highly drought tolerant via deep taproot)
  • Light: Full Sun
  • Soil: Medium-textured, well-drained soils; sensitive to high acidity (prefers pH 6.0–8.0)
  • Habitat: Disturbed areas, roadsides, agricultural fields, waste sites

Distribution

  • Distribution: Introduced from southwestern Asia; now naturalized widely throughout North America (including Canada and Mexico)
  • Seen: Barstow, CA and Mount Charleston, NV
  • Elevation: Below 4,500 ft (1,372 m) overall; grows below 2,450 ft (747 m) in California
