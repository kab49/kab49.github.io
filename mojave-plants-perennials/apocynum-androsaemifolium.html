Spreading Dogbane

Apocynum androsaemifolium, commonly known as spreading dogbane, is a captivating native perennial renowned for its delicate, bell-shaped pink-and-white flowers that emit a sweet fragrance reminiscent of lilacs. Beneath its beautiful exterior, however, lies an advanced evolutionary defense system: a bitter, milky latex sap filled with toxic cardiac glycosides that keep herbivores at bay while serving as a vital nectar oasis for butterflies and specialized moths.

Indigenous peoples throughout North America held this plant in high regard for both its physical utility and medicinal capabilities. The inner bark yields remarkably tough, durable fibers that were traditionally harvested and twisted into fine threads, strong cordage, fishing lines, and bowstrings. Medicinally, various tribes prepared precise root decoctions as a cardiotonic, diuretic, or remedy for headaches and colds, though its high toxicity required immense caution.

Plant Data

Names and Taxonomy

  • Common Names: Spreading Dogbane, Flytrap Dogbane, Bitterroot Dogbane
  • Synonyms: Apocynum ambigens, Apocynum pumilum, Apocynum scopulorum, Apocynum androsaemifolium var. pumilum
  • Family: Apocynaceae
  • Genus Information: CA: 3, NV: 3, AZ: 3, NM: 3, UT: 3, CO: 3

Habit and Size

  • Duration: Perennial
  • Type: Herb
  • Habit: Erect / Sprawling
  • Shape: Rounded / Shrub-like
  • Size: 1–3 ft x 2–3 ft
  • Stem: Smooth, distinctly reddish stems that branch multiple times lacking a single central axis, exuding milky white sap when broken

Leaves

  • Color: Bright green above, lighter pale green or pubescent beneath
  • Shape: Ovate to oblong-elliptic, often with a small sharp point at the tip
  • Edge: Entire
  • Ligule-Petiole:
  • Arrangement: Opposite
  • Leaf Retention: Deciduous

Flowers and Fruit

  • Sex: Hermaphroditic
  • Flower Color: White to light pink with deeper pink stripes inside
  • Flower Size: 1/4 to 1/3 in (5–7 mm) long
  • Number of Petals: 5 petals fused into a tubular bell-shaped corolla with recurved/spreading lobes
  • Bloom: June–August
  • Fruit: A dangling pair of slender, cylindrical, slightly curved follicles containing small seeds with a tuft of silky cottony hairs

Habitat and Growing Conditions

  • Water Use: Medium
  • Light: Sun / Part Shade / Shade
  • Soil: Sandy / Rocky / Gravelly, well-draining
  • Habitat: Forest openings, dry woodlands, forest edges, meadows, and rocky slopes

Distribution

  • Distribution: Widespread across most of North America, stretching from Alaska and Canada south into the mountains of Arizona, California, and northern Mexico
  • Seen: In thin, rocky soils along forest clearings and margins
  • Elevation: 700 ft (213 m) to 9,000 ft (2,743 m)
