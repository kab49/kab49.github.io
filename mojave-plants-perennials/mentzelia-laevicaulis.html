Giant Blazingstar

Giant Blazingstar

Mentzelia laevicaulis, known as the giant blazingstar, is a spectacular wildflower celebrated for its luminous, star-shaped yellow blossoms that expand up to 6 inches across. Its dramatic flowers open primarily at dusk and overnight, acting as a glowing beacon for nocturnal pollinators like hawkmoths, before closing under the afternoon sun. Complementing this stunning display is the plant's unique 'nature's Velcro' foliage—covered in microscopic, barbed hairs that effortlessly cling to clothing and animal fur.

Indigenous peoples held this plant in high regard for its versatile medicinal properties. The Cheyenne traditional healers utilized infusions of the roots to alleviate systemic ailments like rheumatism, arthritis, fevers, and the painful swelling associated with mumps and measles, as well as using it as a treatment to prevent thirst. Similarly, the Gosiute people prepared topical root infusions to soothe bruised swellings and reduce localized inflammation.

Plant Data

Names and Taxonomy

  • Common Names: Giant Blazingstar, Smoothstem Blazingstar, Smoothstem Blazing-star, Blazing-star Mentzelia
  • Synonyms: Bartonia laevicaulis, Mentzelia laevicaulis var. acuminata, Nuttallia laevicaulis
  • Family: Loasaceae
  • Genus Information: CA: 36, NV: 27, AZ: 21, UT: 26, NM: 17, CO: 15

Habit and Size

  • Duration: Biennial, Perennial
  • Type: Herb
  • Habit: Erect
  • Shape: Irregular
  • Size: 3' x 2'
  • Stem: Whitish-green, single but highly branched, harshly scabrid-pubescent, becoming smooth relative to upper parts near the base

Leaves

  • Color: silvery-green, whitish-green
  • Shape: Oblanceolate (lower), oblong to ovate-oblong (upper)
  • Edge: Deeply pinnatifid or sawtoothed, wavy-margined
  • Ligule-Petiole:
  • Arrangement: Alternate
  • Leaf Retention: Summer deciduous / dormant

Flowers and Fruit

  • Sex: Hermaphroditic
  • Flower Color: Lemon yellow, shiny yellow surfaces
  • Flower Size: 1.5 to 6 inches in diameter
  • Number of Petals: 5 narrow, pointed, oblong-lanceolate petals
  • Bloom: June–October
  • Fruit: Woody, cylindrical capsule 1.5–3.5 cm long containing flat, winged seeds

Habitat and Growing Conditions

  • Water Use: Low
  • Light: Full Sun
  • Soil: Sandy, Rocky, Gravelly, Well-draining
  • Habitat: Barren slopes, washes, roadcuts, disturbed gravel areas, and plains

Distribution

  • Distribution: Western North America from British Columbia south through Washington, Oregon, and California, and east to Idaho, Montana, Wyoming, Nevada, Utah, and Colorado
  • Seen: Location or date of observation
  • Elevation: 100 ft (30 m) to 8,850 ft (2,700 m)
