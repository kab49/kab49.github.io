Desert Milkweed

Desert Milkweed

An imposing and resilient desert native, Asclepias erosa stands out with its stout, architectural stems and striking clusters of cream-to-yellowish flowers. Beyond its dramatic structural presence in arid landscapes, this robust perennial serves as a vital ecological lifeline, acting as a critical host plant for monarch butterfly caterpillars and attracting a diverse array of regional pollinators with its copious nectar.

Indigenous peoples of the Southwest borderlands utilized the plant extensively; many cultures, including the Navajo (Diné), Zuni, and Southern Paiute, traditionally collected the white latex sap from buds and stems, hardening it over fires or via other methods to use as a natural chewing gum. Additionally, the tough, fibrous stems of the milkweed were processed into high-quality cordage and string, which the Diné and Zuni used to weave clothing components or secure ceremonial feathers.

Plant Data

Names and Taxonomy

  • Common Names: “Desert Milkweed, California Desert Milkweed”
  • Synonyms: “Asclepias erosa var. erosa”
  • Family: Apocynaceae
  • Genus Information: AZ: 29, CA: 18, NV: 13, UT: 17

Habit and Size

  • Duration: Perennial
  • Type: “Herb”
  • Habit: Erect
  • Shape: Rounded / Irregular
  • Size: 3' - 6' x 2' - 4'
  • Stem: Thick, yellow-green to green, heavily robust at the base, unbranched or lightly branched, often covered in fine cream-colored hairs when young.

Leaves

  • Color: Pale whitish-green to dark green with conspicuous white veining
  • Shape: Ovate, Lanceolate
  • Edge: Entire, wavy margins bending upward toward the central vein
  • Ligule-Petiole:
  • Arrangement: Opposite
  • Leaf Retention: Drought-deciduous

Flowers and Fruit

  • Sex: Hermaphroditic
  • Flower Color: White, cream, or pale yellow-green
  • Flower Size: Umbel clusters 2 - 3 inches in diameter; individual flowers approx. 1/4 inch
  • Number of Petals: 5 reflexed petals with sickle-shaped horns protruding from prominent hoods
  • Bloom: May–October
  • Fruit: Stout, thick, smooth to finely hairy follicles (pods) 2–3 inches long containing flat seeds with cottony tufts

Habitat and Growing Conditions

  • Water Use: Low
  • Light: Full Sun
  • Soil: Sandy, rocky, or well-draining dry granite and clay with low organic content
  • Habitat: Desert washes, dry slopes, mesas, roadsides, and creosote bush scrub

Distribution

  • Distribution: Southwestern United States (California, Arizona, Nevada, Utah) and northern Baja California
  • Seen: Location or date of observation
  • Elevation: 200 ft (60 m) – 5,000 ft (1,524 m)
