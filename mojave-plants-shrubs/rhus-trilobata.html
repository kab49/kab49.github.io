Rhus trilobata

Skunkbush Sumac

A resilient and adaptable western native shrub, Rhus trilobata stands out for its multi-seasonal appeal and ecological significance. Celebrated for its glossy green summer foliage that transforms into blankets of yellow, orange, and vibrant red-purple in autumn, this hardy shrub provides vital cover and nutrient-rich berries for local wildlife throughout the harsher winter months.

Native American tribes heavily relied on this versatile plant for both practical and medicinal applications. The flexible, sturdy split stems were universally prized for fine basketry, while the acidic, citrus-flavored berries were gathered to steep into refreshing lemonade-like drinks, grind into flour for porridge, or mix into traditional breads. Medicinally, infusions of the roots and bark were used to treat ailments ranging from colds and tuberculosis to smallpox and gastrointestinal issues.

Plant Data

Names and Taxonomy

  • Common Names: Skunkbush Sumac, Three-leaf Sumac, Sourberry, Lemonade-bush, Basket-bush
  • Synonyms: Rhus aromatica var. trilobata, Schmaltzia trilobata
  • Family: Anacardiaceae
  • Genus Information: AZ 7, CA 5, NM 5, NV 2, TX 7, UT 3

Habit and Size

  • Duration: Perennial
  • Type: Shrub
  • Habit: Erect / Sprawling
  • Shape: Rounded / Moundlike
  • Size: 3-8 ft x 6-10 ft
  • Stem: Young bark is red-brown or pale gray-brown with pale hairs; older bark is gray-brown, smooth, and develops minor fissures with age; twigs exude a distinct pungent odor when bruised or broken.

Leaves

  • Color: Dark green and shiny above during summer, turning yellow, orange, or red-purple in the fall.
  • Shape: Trifoliate (compound with 3 leaflets), leaflets are unstalked and ovate to rhomboid.
  • Edge: Coarsely-toothed, lobed, or serrate.
  • Ligule-Petiole:
  • Arrangement: Alternate
  • Leaf Retention: Deciduous

Flowers and Fruit

  • Sex: Polygamodioecious (bearing both unisexual and bisexual flowers on the same plant)
  • Flower Color: Yellowish to creamy white
  • Flower Size: Small, approximately 1/4 inch in diameter
  • Number of Petals: 5 rounded petals
  • Bloom: Early Spring (February–April, appearing prior to leaf development)
  • Fruit: Fuzzy, orange-red, sticky, round drupes growing in terminal clusters

Habitat and Growing Conditions

  • Water Use: Low
  • Light: Full Sun / Part Shade
  • Soil: Sandy / Rocky / Clay Loam / Well-draining
  • Habitat: Canyons, dry rocky slopes, stream banks, washes, and riparian scrub boundaries

Distribution

  • Distribution: Western North America from western Canada south through the western United States into Mexico
  • Seen: Desert foothills and canyon washes
  • Elevation: 3,000 to 10,000 ft (914 to 3,048 m)
