Sambucus cerulea

Blue Elderberry

The blue elderberry is a remarkable and highly versatile western native that serves as a cornerstone of riparian ecosystems. Notable for its large, flat-topped clusters of creamy white flowers that transform into heavy, hanging bunches of powder-blue berries, this plant is as visually stunning as it is ecologically valuable. It acts as an oasis for local wildlife, attracting countless pollinators and providing a vital food source for dozens of bird and mammal species.

Highly prized by indigenous peoples of western North America, including the Cahuilla and Coast Salish, the blue elderberry served numerous functional and medicinal purposes. The sweet berries were cooked or dried for food and winter storage, and also processed into rich dyes for basketry. Its soft, pithy wood was easily hollowed out to fashion tools such as arrows, whistles, flutes, and clapper sticks. Medicinally, bark and root infusions were applied as antiseptic washes or poultices for inflammations and boils, while flower extracts were brewed to treat fevers.

Plant Data

Names and Taxonomy

  • Common Names: Blue Elderberry, Blue Elder, Blueberry Elder, Arizona Elderberry, New Mexican Elderberry
  • Synonyms: Sambucus nigra subsp. caerulea, Sambucus caerulea, Sambucus mexicana
  • Family: Viburnaceae
  • Genus Information: AZ: 2, CA: 2, CO: 2, NM: 2, NV: 2, UT: 2

Habit and Size

  • Duration: Perennial
  • Type: Shrub, Tree
  • Habit: Erect
  • Shape: Rounded / Irregular
  • Size: 15 ft x 15 ft to 30 ft x 30 ft
  • Stem: Young twigs soft and pithy, older bark thin and grayish to dark brown with irregular furrows and ridges

Leaves

  • Color: bright green to bluish-green
  • Shape: Ovate to lanceolate, pinnately compound with 5-9 leaflets
  • Edge: Serrated
  • Ligule-Petiole:
  • Arrangement: Opposite
  • Leaf Retention: Deciduous (often summer-deciduous in low desert areas)

Flowers and Fruit

  • Sex: Hermaphroditic
  • Flower Color: White to cream
  • Flower Size: Flat-topped clusters up to 10 inches (25 cm) across; individual flowers small
  • Number of Petals: 5-lobed
  • Bloom: May–September
  • Fruit: Clusters of berry-like drupes, blue- to purple-black with a white-waxy bloom (appearing powder blue)

Habitat and Growing Conditions

  • Water Use: Moderate
  • Light: Full Sun / Part Shade
  • Soil: Sandy / Rocky / Well-draining, moist soil
  • Habitat: Canyons, slopes, riparian areas, open woodlands

Distribution

  • Distribution: Western North America from British Columbia to Baja California, east to the Great Basin and Rocky Mountain foothills
  • Seen:
  • Elevation: 4,500 ft (1,372 m) to 9,500 ft (2,896 m)
