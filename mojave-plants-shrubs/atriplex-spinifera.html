Spiny Saltbush

An enduring symbol of resilience in California's arid interior, Atriplex spinifera distinguishes itself with a formidable labyrinth of rigid, spine-tipped branches. This intricately tangled, pale-gray shrub thrives in harsh, heavily saline alkali sinks, providing vital structural shelter for endangered desert wildlife while beautifully showcasing the survival extremes of desert flora.

Indigenous groups across the western deserts traditionally utilized various species of the saltbush genus for extensive practical and survival needs. The seed-heads were routinely harvested, parched, and ground into a nutritious meal or flour for baking. Beyond nutrition, the alkaline-rich leaves were crushed to create natural, soothing poultices for insect stings and skin irritations, while the dense, durable wood served as an essential fuel source for fires.

Plant Data

Names and Taxonomy

  • Common Names: Spiny Saltbush, Spinescale Saltbush, Mojave Saltbush
  • Synonyms: Atriplex spinifera J.F. Macbr.
  • Family: Chenopodiaceae
  • Genus Information: AZ: 25, CA: 51, NV: 27, NM: 21, UT: 35

Habit and Size

  • Duration: Perennial
  • Type: Shrub
  • Habit: Erect / Sprawling
  • Shape: Irregular
  • Size: 3-6 ft x 4-7 ft
  • Stem: Stiff, highly branched, tangled, and prominently tipped with sharp, woody spiny points; grayish-white texture

Leaves

  • Color: grayish-white, scurfy
  • Shape: Oval to deltoid
  • Edge: Entire
  • Ligule-Petiole:
  • Arrangement: Alternate
  • Leaf Retention: Evergreen to drought-deciduous

Flowers and Fruit

  • Sex: Dioecious
  • Flower Color: Greenish to yellowish-brown
  • Flower Size: Small, inconspicuous
  • Number of Petals: Absent (enclosed in scaly bracts)
  • Bloom: March–May
  • Fruit: Utricle enclosed in specialized scaly fruiting bracts that expand into spherical shapes

Habitat and Growing Conditions

  • Water Use: Low
  • Light: Full Sun
  • Soil: Alkaline, saline, clay, or silty loam
  • Habitat: Alkali sinks, salt flats, lake plains, and alluvial flats

Distribution

  • Distribution: Endemic to California (Central Valley, Mojave Desert, and adjacent inner South Coast Ranges)
  • Seen: Mojave Desert, California
  • Elevation: 1,476–8,202 ft (450–2,500 m)
