Descurainia sophia

Flixweed

Known commonly as flixweed or herb-sophia, Descurainia sophia stands out in the mustard family for its extremely intricate, finely dissected fern-like foliage. Historically revered in Europe as a 'cure-all' for complex wounds and fractures—earning it the Latin moniker *Sophia chirurgorum* or 'wisdom of the surgeons'—this highly adaptable winter annual has successfully naturalized across North American deserts and disturbed landscapes, showcasing extraordinary resilience and prolific seed production.

Historical or indigenous uses. Historically utilized in European folk medicine to treat dysentery, external wounds, and internal bleeding. Following its introduction to North America, the seeds were adopted by Native American tribes, including the Navajo, who roasted and ground them to use as a nutritious flour substitute in baked foods, pinole, and porridge, or as a savory condiment.

Plant Data

Names and Taxonomy

  • Common Names: “Flixweed, Herb Sophia, Flixweed Tansymustard, Flaxweed”
  • Synonyms: “Sisymbrium sophia, Arabis sophia, Sophia sophia”
  • Family: Brassicaceae
  • Genus Information: CA: 8, AZ: 6, NV: 7, UT: 8, NM: 7, CO: 7

Habit and Size

  • Duration: Annual, Biennial
  • Type: “Weed”
  • Habit: Erect
  • Shape: Rounded / Irregular
  • Size: 8-32 in x 6-18 in
  • Stem: Erect, slender, branched distally, covered with branched or stellate hairs, greenish-gray

Leaves

  • Color: gray-green, bright green
  • Shape: Ovate to oblong in outline, 2 to 3 times pinnately compound with linear segments
  • Edge: Lobed, finely dissected
  • Ligule-Petiole:
  • Arrangement: Alternate
  • Leaf Retention: Deciduous

Flowers and Fruit

  • Sex: Hermaphroditic
  • Flower Color: Yellow to greenish-yellow
  • Flower Size: 1/8 inch across
  • Number of Petals: 4 small, oblanceolate petals
  • Bloom: March–July
  • Fruit: Slender, linear siliques (seed pods), 1/2 to 1 1/4 inches long, angled upward

Habitat and Growing Conditions

  • Water Use: Low
  • Light: Full Sun
  • Soil: Sandy / Rocky / Disturbed / Well-draining
  • Habitat: Roadsides, agricultural fields, disturbed desert areas, scrub, grasslands

Distribution

  • Distribution: Widespread throughout the western United States and North America; native to Europe, Asia, and North Africa
  • Seen: Corn Creek
  • Elevation: 3,400 ft (1,036 m) to 11,150 ft (3,400 m)
