Solanum rostratum

Buffalobur Nightshade

An aggressive and fiercely armed native annual, Solanum rostratum is famous for its dense armor of sharp yellow spines covering its stems, leaves, and seed-bearing burs. Known historically for thriving in the heavily disturbed soil of bison wallows, this resilient nightshade features strikingly asymmetrical bright yellow blossoms and demonstrates an ingenious tumbleweed-like mechanism for seed dispersal when mature.

Indigenous groups historically utilized parts of the plant for traditional medicine; notably, some cultures prepared formulations from the plant to treat persistent coughs, while others carefully managed its presence due to its potent, toxic glycoalkaloid properties.

Plant Data

Names and Taxonomy

  • Common Names: Buffalobur, Buffalo Bur, Buffalobur Nightshade, Colorado Bur, Kansas Thistle, Texas Thistle, Horned Nightshade
  • Synonyms: Androcera rostrata, Solanum cornutum, Solanum propinquum
  • Family: Solanaceae
  • Genus Information: AZ: 18, CA: 30, CO: 16, NV: 8, NM: 16, UT: 11

Habit and Size

  • Duration: Annual
  • Type: Weed
  • Habit: Erect / Sprawling
  • Shape: Irregular
  • Size: 2' x 2'
  • Stem: Robust, green to greenish-brown, profusely branching, covered with tiny star-shaped hairs and fiercely armed with straight yellow spines

Leaves

  • Color: bright green
  • Shape: Ovate to broadly elliptic in outline, deeply pinnately lobed
  • Edge: Lobed
  • Ligule-Petiole:
  • Arrangement: Alternate
  • Leaf Retention: Deciduous

Flowers and Fruit

  • Sex: Hermaphroditic
  • Flower Color: Bright yellow
  • Flower Size: 3/4 to 1 inch wide
  • Number of Petals: 5 fused, star-shaped lobes
  • Bloom: May–October
  • Fruit: A dry spherical berry completely enclosed in a fiercely spiny calyx bur

Habitat and Growing Conditions

  • Water Use: Low
  • Light: Full Sun
  • Soil: Sandy / Rocky / Disturbed / Well-draining
  • Habitat: Plains, fields, roadsides, overgrazed pastures, and disturbed places

Distribution

  • Distribution: Widespread across the United States, native to the Great Plains and Southwest, extending into Mexico and Canada
  • Seen: Floyd Lamb Park, NV
  • Elevation: 1,000 to 7,500 ft (305 to 2,286 m); below 1,500 ft (457 m) in California
