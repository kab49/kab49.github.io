Melilotus albus

White Sweetclover

Melilotus albus, commonly known as white sweetclover, stands out in the legume family as a remarkably resilient and fragrant biennial herb. Renowned for its tall, airy racemes of snow-white, pea-like flowers, it fills the air with a powerful, sweet honey-like scent that acts as an irresistible beacon for local pollinators. Beyond its aromatic charm, this species serves as an exceptional nitrogen-fixer, aggressively revitalizing poor or disturbed soils and laying ecological foundations that allow subsequent plant communities to thrive.

Historically introduced as a highly valued agricultural forage crop and a potent honey-producing plant, Melilotus albus also holds deep roots in international folk medicine and ethnobotanical traditions. Indigenous and rural populations, particularly across Europe and parts of Asia, have long utilized its leaves and flowering tops in traditional herbal preparations to treat wounds, inflammatory conditions, and swelling. Due to its high concentrations of aromatic coumarins and beneficial phenolic compounds, infusions and poultices were traditionally applied as emollients to soothe skin ulcers, relieve abdominal discomfort, and serve as an anthelmintic remedy.

Plant Data

Names and Taxonomy

  • Common Names: White Sweetclover, White Melilot, Honey Clover, Bokhara Clover
  • Synonyms: Melilotus officinalis var. albus
  • Family: Fabaceae
  • Genus Information: AZ: 3, CA: 4, NV: 3, UT: 3, NM: 3

Habit and Size

  • Duration: Biennial
  • Type: Weed
  • Habit: Erect
  • Shape: Irregular
  • Size: 3' x 2'
  • Stem: Glabrous to sparsely pubescent, green, multi-branched from a deep taproot

Leaves

  • Color: Bright green
  • Shape: Trifoliate, oblong to obovate leaflets
  • Edge: Serrated
  • Ligule-Petiole:
  • Arrangement: Alternate
  • Leaf Retention: Deciduous

Flowers and Fruit

  • Sex: Hermaphroditic
  • Flower Color: White
  • Flower Size: 1/8 to 1/4 inch (4–6 mm) long
  • Number of Petals: 5 pea-like petals (banner, wings, and keel)
  • Bloom: May–September
  • Fruit: Small, ovoid, reticulated, single-seeded legume pod

Habitat and Growing Conditions

  • Water Use: Low
  • Light: Full Sun
  • Soil: Sandy, rocky, or clayey; highly adaptable to alkaline soils
  • Habitat: Roadsides, river floodplains, disturbed pastures, and waste areas

Distribution

  • Distribution: Widespread throughout the Southwest United States and naturalized across North America
  • Seen: Ash Meadows, CA
  • Elevation: 0 ft (0 m) to 8,000 ft (2,438 m)
