Caulanthus lasiophyllus

California Mustard

An ecologically versatile member of the mustard family, Caulanthus lasiophyllus is celebrated for its distinctive downward-curving seed pods and slender growth form, bringing delicate cream-colored blooms to arid landscapes across western North America each spring.

Historically, indigenous groups such as the Chumash people harvested and utilized the highly nutritious seeds of this native mustard as a seasonal food source.

Plant Data

Names and Taxonomy

  • Common Names: “California Mustard, Slenderpod Jewelflower, Coast Range Wild Cabbage”
  • Synonyms: “Guillenia lasiophylla, Thelypodium lasiophyllum”
  • Family: Brassicaceae
  • Genus Information: AZ 1, CA 14, NV 6, NM 0, UT 4

Habit and Size

  • Duration: Annual
  • Type: “Herb”
  • Habit: Erect
  • Shape: Irregular
  • Size: 7 to 39 inches x 6 to 12 inches
  • Stem: Thin, erect, unbranched or branched distally, sparsely to densely covered by short, stiff hairs at the base

Leaves

  • Color: green to light green
  • Shape: Oblanceolate to lanceolate
  • Edge: Lobed, irregularly toothed
  • Ligule-Petiole:
  • Arrangement: Alternate
  • Leaf Retention: Deciduous

Flowers and Fruit

  • Sex: Hermaphroditic
  • Flower Color: White to creamy white, rarely pinkish
  • Flower Size: Petals are 2.5 to 6.5 mm long
  • Number of Petals: 4 widely spaced, oval-shaped petals
  • Bloom: March–June
  • Fruit: Flat, narrow silique up to 7 cm long, reflexed or hanging downward from short stalks

Habitat and Growing Conditions

  • Water Use: Low
  • Light: Full Sun
  • Soil: Sandy, Rocky, Well-draining
  • Habitat: Desert flats, gravelly areas, limestone rocks, talus slopes, shrub communities, fields, and disturbed sites

Distribution

  • Distribution: Western North America from British Columbia to northern Mexico, extending east to Utah, Arizona, and New Mexico
  • Seen: Mojave, CA
  • Elevation: 0 to 7,905 ft (0 to 2,410 m)
