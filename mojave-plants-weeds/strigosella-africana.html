Strigosella africana

African Mustard

African mustard (Strigosella africana) is a resilient, cold-hardy annual winter weed that has successfully adapted to the arid and disturbed environments of the American Southwest. While its small, pink-to-lavender blossoms offer a brief splash of color during the early spring bloom, the plant quickly transitions into stiff, persistent skeletons. These dried remains accumulate rapidly in desert shrublands, building up an invasive fuel load that significantly alters native fire regimes and promotes desert wildfires.

Though primarily classified as an invasive agricultural and environmental weed within North America, extracts from Strigosella africana have history in traditional Middle Eastern and Asian folk medicine. The alcoholic extracts of the plant possess documented antimicrobial and antiparasitic properties, historically utilized in indigenous practices to treat topical conditions such as eczema and cutaneous leishmaniasis.

Plant Data

Names and Taxonomy

  • Common Names: African Mustard, Turkish Mustard
  • Synonyms: Malcolmia africana, Hesperis africana, Wilckia africana
  • Family: Brassicaceae
  • Genus Information: Number of Species: CA: 1, NV: 1, UT: 1, AZ: 1, NM: 1

Habit and Size

  • Duration: Annual
  • Type: Weed
  • Habit: Erect / Sprawling
  • Shape: Irregular
  • Size: 12-18" x 9-12"
  • Stem: Stiff, branched from the base, and densely covered with small, rough, forked hairs.

Leaves

  • Color: Bright green
  • Shape: Oblanceolate / Elongate
  • Edge: Irregularly dentate / Toothed
  • Ligule-Petiole:
  • Arrangement: Alternate / Basal
  • Leaf Retention: Deciduous

Flowers and Fruit

  • Sex: Hermaphroditic
  • Flower Color: Pink / Lavender / Violet
  • Flower Size: 0.25 to 0.38 inches
  • Number of Petals: 4 petals arranged in a cross shape
  • Bloom: March–July
  • Fruit: Narrow, four-angled, hairy silique pod up to 2 inches long

Habitat and Growing Conditions

  • Water Use: Low / Moderate
  • Light: Full Sun
  • Soil: Sandy / Rocky / Well-draining, thrives in disturbed soil
  • Habitat: Roadsides, foothills, waste places, and creosote bush scrub communities

Distribution

  • Distribution: Widespread across the western United States (including CA, NV, UT, AZ, NM); native to North Africa, the Mediterranean, and parts of central Asia.
  • Seen: Mojave Desert regional roadsides
  • Elevation: 4,000 ft (1,219 m) to 6,000 ft (1,828 m)
