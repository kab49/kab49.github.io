Brassica tournefortii

Sahara Mustard

An aggressive and highly adaptable winter annual, Sahara mustard dominates arid desert landscapes, outcompeting native wildflowers by rapidly forming dense basal rosettes and monopolizing vital soil moisture and light early in the growing season.

Native to North Africa and the Mediterranean, the young leaves and flowers of this species have historically been harvested as edible wild greens, while its nutrient-rich seeds were traditionally gathered and ground into culinary spice pastes or used for oil extraction.

Plant Data

Names and Taxonomy

  • Common Names: Sahara Mustard, African Mustard, Asian Mustard, Tournefort's Birdrape, Wild Turnip
  • Synonyms: Brassica tournefortii var. sisymbrioides, Coincya tournefortii
  • Family: Brassicaceae
  • Genus Information: CA: 7, AZ: 5, NV: 4, UT: 4, NM: 4

Habit and Size

  • Duration: Annual
  • Type: Weed
  • Habit: Erect
  • Shape: Irregular
  • Size: 4 to 46 inches tall
  • Stem: Erect, simple to many-branched above, with the lower portions characteristically covered in coarse, stiff simple hairs

Leaves

  • Color: Bright green to dark green
  • Shape: Lyrate-pinnatifid
  • Edge: Lobed and serrate-toothed
  • Ligule-Petiole:
  • Arrangement: Alternate / Basal Rosette
  • Leaf Retention: Drought-deciduous

Flowers and Fruit

  • Sex: Hermaphroditic
  • Flower Color: Dull pale yellow
  • Flower Size: 0.25 inches in diameter
  • Number of Petals: 4 free petals
  • Bloom: February–April
  • Fruit: Linear silique capsule up to 2.5 inches long, featuring rounded edges and a distinct, prominent terminal beak

Habitat and Growing Conditions

  • Water Use: Low
  • Light: Full Sun
  • Soil: Sandy / Rocky / Well-draining
  • Habitat: Desert dunes, sand ramps, wide washes, roadsides, and disturbed creosote bush scrub communities

Distribution

  • Distribution: Native to North Africa, Southern Europe, and Western Asia; highly invasive and naturalized across the Southwestern United States
  • Seen: Laske Mead, NV
  • Elevation: 0 to 5,500 ft (0 to 1,676 m)
