Melilotus indicus

Annual Yellow Sweetclover

Melilotus indicus, commonly known as annual yellow sweetclover, is a fascinating and highly adaptable member of the pea family that has successfully naturalized across global arid and agricultural landscapes. Highly attractive to honeybees, this plant plays a subtle yet significant role in local ecosystems by actively improving soil health; its symbiotic relationship with specialized root bacteria allows it to fix atmospheric nitrogen, naturally boosting soil fertility in nutrient-poor environments.

Historically originating in the Mediterranean region and Eurasia, Melilotus indicus has a long-standing record of traditional utility. Since ancient times, it has been employed in traditional folk medicine as an emollient, analgesic, astringent, and antispasmodic agent. Additionally, archaeological evidence suggests it has interacted with human agricultural landscapes for millennia, with historical populations valuing it as an aromatic forage crop and green manure, even though modern agriculturalists must manage it carefully to prevent livestock bloating.

Plant Data

Names and Taxonomy

  • Common Names: Annual Yellow Sweetclover, Sour Clover, Indian Sweet-clover, Alfalfilla, Small-flowered Melilot
  • Synonyms: Melilotus parviflorus, Trifolium indicum
  • Family: Fabaceae
  • Genus Information: CA: 4, NV: 3, AZ: 3, UT: 3, NM: 3

Habit and Size

  • Duration: Annual, Biennial
  • Type: Herb
  • Habit: Erect
  • Shape: Irregular
  • Size: 20 in x 12 in (51 cm x 30 cm)
  • Stem: Erect to ascending, many-branched from the base, initially ridged, shiny, and hairless (glabrous)

Leaves

  • Color: Bright green
  • Shape: Pinnately ternate, leaflets oblanceolate to obovate
  • Edge: Serrated above the middle, lower half entire
  • Ligule-Petiole:
  • Arrangement: Alternate
  • Leaf Retention: Deciduous

Flowers and Fruit

  • Sex: Hermaphroditic
  • Flower Color: Yellow
  • Flower Size: Less than 2.5 mm long, arranged in elongated, slender racemes
  • Number of Petals: 5 pea-like petals (banner, wings, and keel)
  • Bloom: April–July
  • Fruit: Small, subglobose, single-seeded, wrinkled pod

Habitat and Growing Conditions

  • Water Use: Low / Moderate
  • Light: Full Sun
  • Soil: Sandy / Rocky / Disturbed, tolerates nutrient-poor soils
  • Habitat: Open disturbed areas, roadsides, fields, and agricultural ditches

Distribution

  • Distribution: Introduced from Eurasia; widely naturalized across the southwestern United States and various global agricultural regions
  • Seen: Corn Creek, NV
  • Elevation: Below 4,500 ft (1,372 m) (tolerates an overall range of -55 ft (-18 m) to 8,495 ft (2,590 m) in coastal and valley conditions)
