Bassia hyssopifolia

Five-horn Smotherweed

An opportunistic survivor of the Amaranth family, Bassia hyssopifolia stands out for its remarkable tolerance to highly saline and alkaline soils where few other plants can take hold. Famously known for its dense, woolly-haired flower spikes and distinctive five-hooked mature seed heads, this tenacious annual efficiently reclaims disturbed arid landscapes, serving as both an aggressive colonizer and a resilient testament to desert adaptation.

While native to Eurasia and largely introduced as an exotic weed across the American West, species within this genus have historically been utilized in pastoral contexts. Indigenous communities and later settlers recognized its utility as an emergency drought-resistant forage for livestock, particularly sheep, despite its potential toxicity from saponins and oxalates if overgrazed. Some closely related species in the Amaranthaceae family were traditionally utilized by Great Basin and Southwestern tribes for their nutritious greens and high-protein seeds during times of scarcity.

Plant Data

Names and Taxonomy

  • Common Names: “Five-horn Smotherweed, Five-hook Bassia, Thorn Orache”
  • Synonyms: “Salsola hyssopifolia, Kochia hyssopifolia”
  • Family: Amaranthaceae
  • Genus Information: AZ: 3, CA: 3, NV: 3, UT: 3, NM: 3

Habit and Size

  • Duration: Annual
  • Type: Weed
  • Habit: Erect
  • Shape: Rounded / Irregular
  • Size: 1–3 ft x 1–2 ft
  • Stem: Erect, highly branched, covered in fine woolly or long hairs; light green to yellowish-green, becoming purplish-red in autumn.

Leaves

  • Color: bright green, gray-green
  • Shape: Linear-lanceolate
  • Edge: Entire
  • Ligule-Petiole:
  • Arrangement: Alternate
  • Leaf Retention: Deciduous

Flowers and Fruit

  • Sex: Hermaphroditic
  • Flower Color: Inconspicuous green
  • Flower Size: 2 mm across
  • Number of Petals: 0 (flowers possess a 5-lobed calyx instead of true petals)
  • Bloom: July–October
  • Fruit: Utricle enclosed by a persistent calyx featuring 5 prominent hooked spines

Habitat and Growing Conditions

  • Water Use: Low
  • Light: Full Sun
  • Soil: Sandy / Rocky / Alkaline / Well-draining
  • Habitat: Disturbed areas, roadsides, ditches, alkaline flats, playas, creosote bush scrub

Distribution

  • Distribution: Widespread throughout Western North America, including California, Nevada, Arizona, Utah, and New Mexico; native to Eurasia.
  • Seen: Observed in alkaline washes and disturbed roadsides. Ash Meadows...Crystal Reservoir
  • Elevation: 330 ft (100 m) to 6,650 ft (2,026 m)
